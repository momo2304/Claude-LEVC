## 2. Regulation that decides the car

- **London ZEC rule:** since 1 Jan 2023 every PHV licensed for the first time must be zero emission capable (≤50 g/km CO2 with 10+ miles zero-emission range, or ≤75 g/km with 20+ miles) and Euro 6. **Plug-in hybrids like S580e, E300e, 750e qualify; diesel V-Class (V220d/V300d) and 740d cannot be newly licensed as a London PHV.** EQV, EQE, EQS, i7 qualify.
- **London age limit:** PHVs can be re-licensed up to 10 years from first registration.
- **Signage / disc exemption** for chauffeur work: per-vehicle, discretionary, annual (form PHV/302); no price threshold or model list published. Owner-drivers must prove chauffeur-only work.
- **Congestion Charge:** £18/day from 2 Jan 2026; EV discount now 25% (£13.50) on Auto Pay only; blanket EV exemption ended 25 Dec 2025.
- **Outside London:** Greater Manchester requires Euro 4 petrol / Euro 6 diesel / ZEC by 31 Dec 2026; Leeds executive PHVs must be on an approved list, under 1 year old at first licensing, max 5 years; Birmingham CAZ £8/day for non-compliant cars; Edinburgh LEZ since Jun 2024.
- **National reform:** Draft Taxi and PHV Bill (King's Speech May 2026); DfT consultation (Jan–Apr 2026) on cutting licensing authorities from 263 to ~70 to curb out-of-area working.
- **Tax:** EV Expensive Car Supplement threshold £50k for zero-emission cars registered from 1 Apr 2025 (still £40k for PHEVs); 100% first-year allowance for **new** zero-emission cars to Mar/Apr 2027 (not used cars); main pool WDA now 14%; eVED 3p/mile EV and 1.5p/mile PHEV from April 2028; MTD for Income Tax from 6 Apr 2026 for sole traders over £50k.
- **VAT:** PH operators removed from TOMS from 2 Jan 2026 (20% VAT on full fare for principal operators); Supreme Court *Uber v Sefton* (Jul 2025) keeps agency model lawful outside London.
- **Market size (DfT, 1 Apr 2026):** 290,000 licensed PHVs in England (+13% since 2024), 93,300 in London; 296,800 PHV driver licences.

## 3. What drivers say (forums, reviews, competitors)

Access: Reddit, UKPHD and Facebook groups could not be read (blocked or login-walled). PistonHeads, TaxiPoint, Trustpilot, UberPeople snippets and competitor pages were read.

- **Costs vs earnings:** "you're earning £140 for the day, less your running costs" (PistonHeads); exec drivers warn of ~£7k/yr depreciation, ~£3k/yr insurance, ~£5k/yr finance on an E-Class (UberPeople, unverified).
- **Finance for hire and reward:** many standard HP/PCP agreements exclude taxi/PHV use and cap mileage; drivers seek HP with a large deposit and no mileage cap.
- **Rental distrust (strongest signal):** deposits not returned, contracts not signed, write-off settlements kept by the rental firm, multiple trading names (Trustpilot reviews of Zoom PCO Hire 1.6/5, PCO Rentals 3.2/5; Otto Car 4.3/5 with 13% one-star).
- **Models:** EQE is the volume Exec EV; EQS stacks Uber Lux + Wheely + Blacklane; S-Class is the only Wheely First car; V-Class/EQV for airport and group work; i7 pushed by rental firms; little evidence on E300e.
- **Competitor weekly prices (rent / rent-to-buy):** Splend EQE £355/wk incl. insurance (£1,999 fee); Otto EQE £329, i5 £349 incl. insurance; Zoom PCO EQE £229 excl. insurance, i7 £399 excl. insurance (£5,899 deposit); Electric PCO EQS £520; Stallion V-Class £446–546 excl. insurance.
- **Trust builders:** clear terms in writing, finance in the driver's own name (they own the car and any insurance payout), no mileage cap tied to hire and reward, honest one trading name, eligibility checked before purchase.
- **Vocabulary:** PCO, PHV, Exec, Lux, XL, Wheely Business/First, Blacklane, hire and reward, rent-to-buy, "no balloon", plate years ("24 plate", "75 plate"), "showroom standard", airport jobs, Auto Pay, ex-chauffeur.

## 6. Finance landscape

- **Haydock Finance:** prestige vehicles via HP / HP with balloon up to 5 years, refinance; for high-net-worth individuals and limited companies, **advances over £70,000, non-regulated only**. Broker/partner route. ([Haydock prestige](https://www.haydockfinance.co.uk/sectors/prestige-vehicles))
- **Close Brothers:** Motor Finance (regulated point-of-sale, dealers must be FCA authorised) and Asset Finance (business HP, lease, refinance). FY26: £320m motor-finance redress provision held, dividend suspended. ([Close Motor Finance dealers](https://www.closemotorfinance.co.uk/become-a-dealer-partner) · [FY26](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/close-brothers-fy26-slides-cost-cuts-deliver-as-profit-falls-17-93CH-4921936))
- **Supreme Court (1 Aug 2025):** no fiduciary duty for brokers, but undisclosed high/tied commission can be unfair under s140A CCA. **FCA redress scheme PS26/3** (30 Mar 2026) covers regulated agreements 2007–2024; parts suspended by the Upper Tribunal on 2 Jul 2026. ([Kirkland](https://www.kirkland.com/publications/kirkland-alert/2025/08/motor-finance-uk-supreme-court-allows-lenders-appeal-in-large-part) · [FCA](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/statements/fca-confirms-motor-finance-redress-scheme))
- **Business exemption:** credit over £25,000 wholly or mainly for business, with a declaration, is exempt; sole traders and small partnerships borrowing £25,000 or less are regulated; limited companies are outside consumer credit regulation. ([Blake Morgan](https://blakemorgan.co.uk/the-business-exemption-under-the-consumer-credit-act))
- **Commission disclosure** (CONC 4.5.3R) applies to regulated deals: disclose existence and nature of commission prominently.
- **Other players:** Liberty Finance (formerly Liberty Leasing; prestige/fleet HP £10k–£2m); Zuto broker panel; rent-to-buy (Splend, Otto); Islamic options (Ayan, Mizan Finance); PCO brokers typically show ~10% deposit over 36–60 months.
- **Implication for the site:** most nearly new exec cars are over £25k, so a sole trader buying for business can use the business exemption, and limited companies are unregulated. Consumer-regulated deals still need full FCA treatment. Finance copy stays off until approved wording is set.

- **Legal basis:** "triple lock" (driver, vehicle and operator from the same authority). A council-licensed car can lawfully do London journeys if the booking was accepted by an operator licensed by that council (PHV (London) Act 1998 s6(6)(b)); subcontracting under 1976 Act s55A.
- **"Dual licensing" in practice:** drivers hold driver licences with more than one authority; each car is licensed by one (Hertsmere and Wolverhampton forbid a car holding two).
- **Wheely accepts TfL, RBWM, Castle Point and Hertsmere licences** and gives the same jobs whatever the authority; RBWM needs a plate exemption. Uber restricts drivers to their licensing region (long trips allowed).
- **Risk:** Hertsmere's 2025 policy targets operators whose journeys mostly start/end elsewhere; DfT consultation (Jan–Apr 2026) proposes ~70 licensing authorities to curb out-of-area working (London excluded); Draft Taxi and PHV Bill (May 2026).
