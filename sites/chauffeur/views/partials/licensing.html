Licensing

TfL, a council, or both?

Many chauffeurs who work in London license with a council such as Hertsmere or Windsor and Maidenhead, often alongside a TfL driver licence. The car you can buy depends on which authority licenses it, so we start there.

London

TfL

  • First licence: zero emission capable only (EV or a plug-in hybrid such as the S 580 e).
  • Re-licensed up to 10 years old.
  • Needed for most London platform work, including Uber in London.
Hertfordshire

Hertsmere

  • Up to 6 years old at first licence; Euro 6; no zero emission rule.
  • Plate exemption possible for executive and corporate contract work.
  • A car licensed by Hertsmere can’t also be licensed elsewhere.
Berkshire

Windsor & Maidenhead

  • Up to 5 years old at first licence, 12 maximum; exceptions for prestige cars.
  • Plate exemption with written business contracts.
  • Wheely accepts RBWM licences with a plate exemption.
Why it matters for your S-Class: Wheely accepts TfL, Hertsmere, Windsor & Maidenhead and Castle Point licences, and gives chauffeurs the same jobs whichever authority licensed them. Because those councils don’t apply TfL’s zero emission rule, a nearly new diesel S 350 d L or S 400 d L can be an option for Wheely First, often for less than an S 580 e. The driver, car and booking operator must be licensed by the same authority. Councils are tightening rules on out-of-area working, so always check the current policy with the council and the platform before you buy.
